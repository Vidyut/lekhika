--- title: What Lekhak is kind: scene page_break: auto ---

Lekhak is a writing program for long work: novels, essays, anything with chapters that grow, or a place to keep and organise all your writing in folders. It keeps your manuscript in ordinary files on your own disk, gives you a place to write without thinking about formatting, and makes it easy to export a finished book when you want one.

The idea is simple: just write. Everything else — how the book is dressed when it leaves Lekhak, whether the pages are A4 or Letter, whether there is a title page — is handled by templates. The default should "just work" for most simple texts exported for online reading. Specific needs, like page sizes for publishing, are easy to configure. Extensive customisation is possible if you need it. Detailed guidance is in the Templates and export section.

As a writer, here is some reassurance I know will matter to writers.

Your writing stays yours. A project is a folder of files you can open, read, copy and back up. The data is always stored so that you can edit the raw files on the disk if you wish. Lekhak never locks your manuscript into a proprietary format.

Lekhak is designed to be intuitive to use, so that most features should be easy to understand and you don't need a doctorate in software configuration to export your own work. If you run into problems, filing a bug on GitHub should get prompt answers.

This guide is also on my blog, where it is updated more often than the copy on your computer, which only changes when the software does. It can be useful to check the blog if you're struggling with something.

By default, your project data does not leave your computer unless you explicitly configure an API, or something of that kind. There are extensive precautions to ensure your data is never deleted or overwritten without your knowledge.
